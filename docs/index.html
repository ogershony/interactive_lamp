How it was built

From someone else's robot to this one

The lamp has no expressive motion of its own and no corpus to learn from. The pipeline borrows both: it takes a published set of robot animations that people have already labelled with emotions, moves the meaning of that motion onto a different body, and trains a generator on the result.

The hard part

Retargeting, and the cost of calm

Cozmo is a wheeled robot with a lift and an animated face. The lamp is a five-joint arm with one LED. No joint maps to another, so the mapping works in feature space: read what the motion means — attention, posture, drive, gaze, brightness — then synthesize a lamp pose that says the same thing with its own body.

The raw mapping asks for 10.21 rad/s — roughly six times what the servos will do. Post-processing lands it exactly on the cap, which is why the corpus is trainable at all.

Five passes over the whole corpus

Each mapping revision was measured against every one of the 926 source clips before it was kept. Two measures, two frames — putting them on one pair of axes would only flatter the result.

Calming is not free. Correlation with the source head channel falls from 0.963 to 0.918 across these runs — the lamp deliberately stops tracking its source exactly, because a lamp that twitches at 4 rad/s reads as broken rather than alive.

Does it work

Evaluated without asking anyone to rate anything

A rater study was out of reach, so the model is gated on measurements that need no raters: can a probe trained on real motion read the generated motion, does intensity move monotonically with guidance, and does every sample survive the validator.

The projection story

Guidance above 1 is what makes affects distinguishable — and it is also what pushes samples past the joint speed limit. At cfg 2.5 the raw model output failed the validator on every one of eight test affects.

Re-applying the mapping's own rate tracker fixed all eight while keeping 99% of mean joint speed. Guidance and projection together took the spread of mean speed across affects from 1.98× to 3.21×, against 3.24× in the real data — so the generated corpus varies by affect almost exactly as much as the real one does.
